## Exam format (~20 min per student, individual)

| # | Part | Time | Points |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Demo**: student runs the deployed app and shows the main flow | 5 min | 20 |
| 2 | **Own code**: 2 questions, you point at a file | 4 min | 20 |
| 3 | **JS fundamentals**: 1 small problem, solved live (paper or browser console), thinking out loud | 5 min | 20 |
| 4 | **Concepts**: 2 questions | 4 min | 25 |
| 5 | **"What if"**: 1 extension / debugging question | 2 min | 15 |
| | **Total** | **~20 min** | **100** |

## Tips for the examiner

- **Start with their own code.** They feel safe there; confidence makes the rest go better.
- **Grade understanding, not syntax.** "I would use filter, it keeps the items where the condition is true" with a small typo = full marks.
- If a student is stuck for >30 s, give the **hint** in the script. A hint costs at most half the points of that question.
- Ask **"Why?"** at least once per part. Copy-pasted code can be demoed, but "why" shows understanding.
- Use **different JS problems** for the two students (the scripts already do).
- Write notes on the grading sheet **during** the exam, not after.
